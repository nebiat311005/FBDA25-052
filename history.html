History & Struggles

The long road to independence and beyond

Eritrea's story includes colonisation, war, displacement and sanctions. Understanding these layers helps explain the challenges many Eritreans still face today.

Key historical moments

  1. Late 1800s -Italian colonisation

    Italy established control over coastal and highland areas, building infrastructure but ruling with colonial policies that limited local power.

  2. 1941-1952 - British administration

    After Italy's defeat in World War II, Eritrea came under British military administration. Debates about its future status began.

  3. 1962 - Annexation by Ethiopia

    Eritrea's federation with Ethiopia was dissolved and the territory was annexed, sparking a 30-year armed struggle for independence.

  4. 1961-1991 - Armed struggle

    Eritrean fighters, many of them young men and women, lived in harsh conditions in the field while civilians faced bombings, displacement, and loss.

  5. 1993 - Independence

    A UN-supervised referendum saw an overwhelming vote for independence, and Eritrea was internationally recognised as a sovereign state.

Post-independence struggles

The 1998-2000 border war with Ethiopia, ongoing tensions, and international sanctions have strained the economy and limited opportunities. Many young people have left the country in search of safety, education, and work.

Access to information and freedom of expression has also been restricted, affecting how stories from inside the country are shared with the world.

Human impact

  • Families separated by migration and conflict.
  • Limited access to quality education and health services in some areas.
  • Pressure of long-term national service on young Eritreans.
  • Psychological impact of war, loss, and uncertainty.
